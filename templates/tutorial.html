How to use Meetinghouse

Meetinghouse is a place where a group of people make decisions together, slowly and in the open, the way a Quaker Meeting for Business does. People talk a matter through, Vote, change their minds, and Vote again, until the Meeting reaches a decision.

This page walks you through the site, step by step. The pictures come from the practice sandbox, where everyone is made up. You can try everything there without changing anything real.

1. Looking around

Anyone can read everything on Meetinghouse, without joining or logging in. On the front page, click Just look around?

The tabs along the top take you to each part of the site. You'll see the open Conversations in each of the three Committees:

  • The Forum Committee decides outside matters: anything the Meeting wants to decide about the world.
  • The Rules Committee decides how Meetinghouse itself works.
  • The Jury Committee decides whether to dismiss a Member for a while, if they've been abusive.

Click a Committee's tab to see all its Conversations, open and closed.

2. Joining

To take part, you join as a Member. Joining takes a few minutes, and nobody has to approve you: the site does it all by itself.

  1. Choose a user name. On the front page, type the user name you'd like in the Log in box. If nobody has it yet, the site asks for your name, email address, town, postal code and country.
  2. Add a passkey. A passkey lets you log in with your phone's or computer's face, fingerprint or PIN unlock, the same way you unlock the device. There's no password to remember or to steal.
  3. Pass the ID check. Choose one of the approved ID-check companies. You photograph your ID, take a short live selfie, and show proof of your address. This proves you're a real person who joins only once. Meetinghouse keeps only the result, your town, postal code and country, and your selfie, which becomes your public photo.
  4. You're in. When the check passes, your membership starts straight away, and a welcome email arrives.

If your photo looks like someone else's: the site compares every new Member's face with everyone's, so nobody can join twice. If yours looks a lot like a Member's, you'll be asked for a new selfie in good light. If it still matches, the Jury Committee looks at both photos and decides. Twins and look-alikes are let in.

Once a year the site asks you to confirm you're still here, with a quick selfie. You'll get reminder emails a month and a week before. If the date passes, you can still read everything, but you can't Vote or comment until you do it.

3. Taking part in a Conversation

Every decision is made in a Conversation. A Conversation has a Title, the words its Initiator started it with, a Discussion, and usually a Question for the Meeting to decide.

When you open a Conversation

Opening a Conversation makes you one of its Contactors. That matters: a Question passes only if enough of its Contactors Vote. So if you open a Conversation, please Vote on its Question. The first time you open one, the site asks you, once:

The Discussion

The Discussion is where the Meeting talks the matter through. Comments and Votes, each with its reason, appear together in the order they were made. Write as much as you like in the box at the bottom and click Post Comment.

Voting

Under the Discussion are the Votes. Choose your Vote, write your reason, and click Vote. Every Vote needs its reason, and everyone can read it.

  • Yes or No.
  • Abstain, in the Forum Committee only, means you stand aside: you won't stand in the way. It counts in favor.
  • You can change your Vote as often as you like; your latest Vote is the one that counts. Changing your mind as the Discussion goes on is the point.

4. How a Conversation closes

Nothing is decided in a hurry. A Conversation closes only when the Meeting has had time to settle:

  1. Once its Question is attached, it stays open for at least 3 weeks (its Minimum Life).
  2. Then it waits until it has gone 3 weeks with nothing new (its Seasoning Time). Each new Comment or Vote starts those 3 weeks over.
  3. When it closes, the Question passes only if enough Contactors Voted (the Threshold), and enough of the Votes were in favor (the Approval). Otherwise it fails.

The 5 restarts. So that nobody can keep a Conversation open forever, only your first 5 Comments and your first 5 Votes in a Conversation start the Seasoning Time over. After that you can still comment and Vote, and your Vote still counts; it just doesn't keep the Conversation open. The note beside each button tells you how many you have left.

A closed Conversation shows its result at the top, and stays on the record for good.

The Current Rules page shows each Committee's Threshold and Approval as they stand today.

5. Starting a Conversation

Any Member can start a Conversation. On your Dashboard, click New Conversation beside the Committee you want, give it a Title, and write what's on your mind.

A Question is optional in the Forum Committee. You can start with Discussion only, and attach a Question later, once the Meeting knows what to ask. Anyone who opens a Conversation that has no Question yet is asked whether they'd like to attach one. Whoever attaches it becomes its Pollster, and the 3 weeks of Minimum Life start then.

6. The Rules and Jury Committees

Changing a Rule

The Rules Committee can change how hard it is for a Question to pass in each Committee: its Threshold or its Approval. Change is slow on purpose: each Rules Vote can move a number by at most 5% of its value, one setting at a time, and every number stays between 9% and 100%. The site lists only the values you're allowed to choose.

When a Rules Conversation passes, the site makes the change itself, at once, and writes it in the public Log. Nobody has to do anything, and nobody can change a Rule any other way.

The Jury

If a Member is abusive, any Member can start a Jury Conversation about them. Jury Votes are Yes or No. If it passes, the Member is dismissed for a while: 2 weeks the first time, then 4, then 8. A Dismissal always ends.

While someone is dismissed, they can't log in, and their Comments and Votes are hidden. Their Votes come off every open Conversation, and each one's Seasoning Time starts over so everyone can see the change. When the time is up, they come back on their own.

7. Groups, and splitting a group

At first, everyone is in one group: Group A, the whole Meetinghouse. When a group feels too big for its Members to know each other, it can split in two, by where people live. When A splits, its halves are AA and AB; when AA splits, AAA and AAB, and so on. You belong to your own group and every group above it, so a Member of AA is also a Member of A.

How a group splits:

  1. Deciding. In a group with at least 30 Members, any of them can propose splitting it, on the Groups tab. That starts a Rules Conversation, “Should Group A split?”, with an ordinary Yes-or-No Vote among the group's Members. While it's being decided, the map of where Members live is frozen.
  2. Suggesting. If it passes, the site draws a circle around each town or cluster of Members, and suggests two ways to put the circles into two halves. Any Member of the group can suggest another way, by ticking which circles go in one half. Each half needs between 40% and 60% of the Members, its circles next to each other, and to reach the edge of the group's area. The site checks each suggestion at once and says if something's wrong.
  3. Rating. Then everyone in the group rates the ways: 1 for their first choice, 2 for their second, and so on, each with a reason. The way more than half prefer wins. If none has more than half at first, the way with the fewest first choices is dropped and those ballots count for their next choice, until one way has more than half.
  4. Done. Each Member is placed in the half they live in. New Members are placed automatically.

After a split, when you start a Forum or Jury Conversation, the site asks which of your groups it's for, and only that group's Members can take part. Choose the smallest group the matter concerns. The Rules stay the same for everyone, decided by all of Group A. If a Conversation is in the wrong group, its Contactors can flag it during its first week, and its Initiator can move it.

8. Stewards

Three Stewards keep the lights on: they pay for the hosting and the meetinghouse.ai name, and keep the server running. They have no other powers. A Stewardship lasts one year, nobody can be a Steward twice, and a new Steward is chosen every four months, so there are always two who already know the job.

The site runs each Steward Vote by itself, in the Rules Committee:

  1. Volunteers. For 3 weeks, any Member who has never been a Steward can put their name forward, with a few words about why. Everyone can comment and ask the volunteers questions.
  2. The Vote. If more people stand than there are places, everyone rates them, 1 for their first choice and so on, the same way as choosing how to split. If no more stand than there are places, it's a Yes-or-No Vote on each. Every ballot shows with its reason.
  3. Thank you. Each volunteer who isn't chosen gets a thank-you note, once, with a box to tick if they'd like to stand again next time.

The first Steward Vote opens a year after Meetinghouse opens to Members. You can find the Stewards, and every Steward Vote, from the Rules Committee page.

9. Keeping watch

The Daily Summary shows, in one place, every open Conversation and how its Votes stand, what closed today, and anyone dismissed today.

The Log records everything the site does: Members joining and leaving, Conversations, Votes, results, Rules changes, Dismissals, and which version of the software is running. Nothing is ever taken out. Each day has a fingerprint: a long code that depends on everything logged so far. If anyone ever changed an old entry, the fingerprints would stop matching, and the Log page would say so.

The Map shows roughly where Members live: each as part of a square about 5 km across, with the town's name. No names, and no addresses.

10. Your account

The Account tab is where you:

  • see when your yearly selfie is due;
  • add a passkey for another phone or computer, or remove one you no longer use;
  • change your email address.

If you lose your device, type your user name on the Log in page, click Lost your device?, and ask for a recovery code by email. For your safety, the code only starts working 48 hours later, and an email goes out at once, so if someone else asked for it, you can cancel it.

Every time you log in, the site tells you when you last logged in. If that wasn't you, use account recovery.

Leaving. You can leave at any time, from the bottom of your Account page. Your Comments and Votes come off every open Conversation; your Comments and Votes in closed Conversations stay on the record under your name, with a plain “left Meetinghouse” picture instead of your photo. Your email address, photo and passkeys are deleted. You're welcome back any time, by joining again.

11. The words we use

Member
Someone who has joined.
Committee
The Forum, Rules or Jury Committee.
Conversation
A matter brought to one of the Committees.
Initiator
The Member who started a Conversation.
Question
What the Meeting is asked to decide in a Conversation.
Pollster
The Member who attached the Question.
Contactor
A Member who has opened a Conversation, commented on it, or Voted on it.
Threshold
How many of the Contactors must Vote for a Question to pass.
Approval
How many of the Votes must be in favor.
Minimum Life
At least 3 weeks: how long a Question stays open.
Seasoning Time
3 weeks without anything new before a Conversation closes.
Dismissal
A Member's time away, decided by the Jury.
Group
Group A is all of Meetinghouse; after a split, AA and AB are its halves, and so on.
Steward
One of the three Members, chosen by Vote for a year, who pay the bills and keep the server running.

For the full design, and the reasons behind it, see How Meetinghouse works.
